# Evidence Redraws the Tanzanian PHP Project Menu

A developer learning PHP and Laravel in Dar es Salaam has plenty of real problems to build for, but the evidence moves them away from handling money and towards keeping records around money. The best-supported problems are shop record keeping and customer debt, savings-group and small-SACCO ledgers, fee ledgers for small schools, order and delivery tracking for social-media sellers, and member and delivery records for farmer cooperatives. Wherever a public service or a payment is collected (bus tickets, city fares, electricity, loans, wallets), a regulator now requires a licence, an approved vendor or integration with a government system, so those ideas are worth building only to learn. Three pieces of infrastructure shape every serious project: mobile money reached through an aggregator, SMS and USSD through a local provider, and TRA fiscal receipts sent by a Virtual Fiscal Device. All three can be practised without a company, but only against sandboxes, simulators and a mock server the learner writes. Going live needs a registered business, a TIN, Personal Data Protection Commission registration and a licensed payment partner. The five-stage menu from the earlier report survives, with one project demoted (bus ticketing), several narrowed (school fees, pharmacy stock, boda dispatch, rent book, VICOBA) and several added (pledge ledger, parcel desk, seller order desk, cooperative records, QR and loan-schedule tools). Much of the evidence is press coverage, search-result summaries or vendor claims; those are marked throughout and should be checked before anyone quotes them. Nothing here is legal advice.

**How to read the evidence marks.** *[summary]* means the fact comes from a search-result summary and the page itself was not opened. *[vendor]* means the company's own claim. *[third-party]* means a blog, SDK README or law-firm note that was not checked against the official text. *[unconfirmed]* means sources conflict or a single weak source carries the claim. *[general knowledge]* means no source was found in this research. Unmarked facts come from pages that were opened and read. Research date: 21 November 2026.

## Record keeping is the open problem; payment collection is the fenced one

### Small shops: the pain is documented, the percentages are not

The only national count of small businesses is fourteen years old: the 2012 baseline survey put the sector at about **3 million MSMEs**, with over 95% of micro businesses operating informally and only 3.9% of small businesses registered with BRELA *[summary, 2012 data]* ([FSDT focus note](https://fsdt.or.tz/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/FSDT-Focus-Note-Business-Formalization-in-Tanzania.pdf)). Studies in Dar es Salaam link poor record keeping to weak growth and to refused bank credit *[summary]* ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/10205383); [COSTECH repository](https://repository.costech.or.tz/items/10f62eb4-a3e4-4843-9bb3-f937d44f5f59)), and a UDSM journal article states that SMEs usually sell on credit *[summary]* ([ORSEA journal](https://journals.udsm.ac.tz/index.php/orsea/article/view/2296/2380)). No study with a headline percentage of shops keeping no records was retrieved. The earlier report's "68% of SMEs keep records by hand" came from press coverage of an FSDT study and could not be confirmed in this round, so treat it as *[unconfirmed]*.

Two pressures make shop records more valuable now. Kariakoo traders' main grievance in 2024 was tax estimated by TRA without data ([The Guardian/IPP](https://ippmedia.com/the-guardian/news/local-news/read/treasury-rejects-replacing-efds-for-kariakoo-traders-2024-06-25-065548)), and a government Order in force from **1 July 2026** requires electronic payment in named sectors (public transport, filling stations, school fees, accommodation, food and beverage and others), with existing businesses given until the end of December 2026 to comply ([EY tax alert](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1753-tanzania-introduces-mandatory-electronic-payment-for-specific-identified-transactions)). A simple ledger of sales, expenses and customer debt answers the owner's question about profit, the bank's question about history and TRA's question about turnover. Software-only tools also avoid the cost that sank the venture-funded distributors: MarketForce closed its small-shop distribution arm in April 2024, including in Tanzania, citing thin margins on fast-moving goods ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/19/marketforce-winds-down-its-b2b-e-commerce-arm/)). No evidence was found on Tanzanian shop bookkeeping apps either way; two searches finding nothing does not prove the market is empty.

### Savings groups and SACCOs: big, mostly on paper at the small end, and dominated by operator wallets at the top

Informal savings groups (VICOBA, VSLA) reach about **12% of adults, roughly 5 million people**, against about 1% for SACCOs *[press report of FSDT/FinScope figures]* ([TanzaniaInvest](https://www.tanzaniainvest.com/finance/digitization-community-microfinance-groups-fsdt)). More than 48,659 groups were registered through the Bank of Tanzania's online system by March 2025 (same source). On the SACCO side, 981 licensed societies held TZS 1.69 trillion in assets at the end of 2025, and **only 268 of them (about one in four) use a core digital system**; bad loans averaged 8.1% against a 5% limit and only 46% of audits were clean ([The Chanzo](https://thechanzo.com/2026/10/15/saccos-assets-hit-sh1-69-trillion-but-bad-loans-breach-5-limit/)). These figures replace the earlier report's "50,000 groups, 4.4 million clients, 5–10% of SACCOs digitised", which this round could not support.

The documented failures are about custody and verification. In Segerea, Dar es Salaam, one group lost a TZS 48 million share-out after members were told the money was banked when it sat in boxes at an official's home, and another lost TZS 100 million *[2018]* ([The Citizen](https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/-how-vicoba-members-found-themselves-sh48m-poorer-2655164)). The market's answer has been group wallets run by a mobile operator with a bank: Vodacom reports about 300,000 active M-Koba groups *[vendor, summary; two counts not reconciled]* ([TechAfricaNews](https://techafricanews.com/2025/08/27/vodacom-tanzania-expands-5g-and-digital-finance-to-drive-national-digital-economy)), with dual sign-off on loans and a notice to every member for each transaction ([Carnegie Endowment](https://carnegieendowment.org/2024/01/08/benefits-and-risks-of-bank-and-savings-group-partnerships-in-tanzania-pub-91327)). A learner cannot compete with that and should not try to hold money. The room left is the record-keeping side (the route CARE's Chomoka app took), groups whose members sit on different networks or want their own rules, and the 713 SACCOs with no core system. No measured data on record errors, default rates or dispute frequency in groups was found.

### Schools, pharmacies and farms: real workflows, crowded or government-owned rails

School fees follow a well-evidenced workflow: instalments (three a year at some Dar es Salaam private schools), a control or reference number per student, and payment through a bank or mobile money *[summary]* ([The Citizen](https://thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/parents-navigate-new-school-term-pressures-amid-extended-hours-5316672); [Daily News](https://dailynews.co.tz/airtel-crdb-launch-fees-payment-platform/)). The space is crowded: ShuleSoft says it serves more than 400 private schools *[vendor, summary]* ([ShuleSoft](https://www.shulesoft.co/resources/requirements/ShuleSoft%20International%20Product%20Proposal.pdf)), and CRDB, Exim, Airtel and Vodacom all sell school-fee products. No current count of private schools was found, so the market cannot be sized. Sending children home over unpaid fees has drawn government directives against it *[summary, older]* ([The Citizen](https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/oped/opinion-private-schools-are-misunderstood-2667612)), so a fees tool should be built around statements, instalment plans and reminders, not exclusion lists.

Private drug shops have a legal hook that generic shop software lacks: the 2019 regulations require accredited outlets (ADDOs) to keep a dispensing register and a separate register of expired products *[summary]* ([TanzLII, GN 185 of 2019](https://tanzlii.org/akn/tz/act/gn/2019/185/eng@2019-03-15/source.pdf)), and there are about 14,000 ADDOs *[summary; date unclear]* ([PLOS Global Public Health via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11482716)). But Maisha Meds gives away an Android point-of-sale used by nearly 5,000 facilities in four countries *[vendor, summary]* ([MIT Solve](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/95587)), and public-sector stock is locked into the government's eLMIS, through which all facility orders must go ([The Citizen](https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/why-drug-shortages-persist-despite-major-health-reforms-5404684)). No study measures expiry losses in private outlets, so the size of the problem is unknown. The best-quantified health pain is insurance claims: NHIF rejected **Sh11.83 billion** of public-hospital claims in 2022/23 *[summary]* ([The Citizen](https://thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/fraud-crisis-nhif-and-health-centres-facing-overwhelming-deception-4990134)). That is a real gap but a hard build that depends on NHIF formats and handles health data.

Agriculture carries the clearest warning. A study counted about **7.2 million farmers registered on the government's M-Kilimo platform but only 75,623 advisory consultations**, roughly one per hundred registered farmers, and farmers interviewed could not name anyone who earned more through it ([Al Jazeera, 6 Nov 2026](https://aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/6/tanzanias-smallholder-farmers-struggle-to-benefit-from-digital-agriculture)). Another farmer-facing price app would repeat that failure. The open niche is one level up, at the primary cooperative (AMCOS): officials blame late crop payments partly on inaccurate farmer bank details *[summary, undated]* ([The Citizen](https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/fresh-concern-over-cashew-payments-2699124)), and in August 2026 the Vice-President ordered tighter cooperative audits to curb embezzlement *[summary]* ([allAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202608040240.html)). A clean member register, delivery records per member and a statement each farmer can check is the tool that fits. Whether an AMCOS would pay for it is unknown.

### Transport, delivery and housing: the regulated areas have the hard numbers

Intercity bus ticketing is now a licensed market. Under the Electronic Ticketing Regulations of 2024, a system must be owned by a government body, a cooperative or a company registered in Tanzania and be integrated with government revenue systems; from **16 February 2026** only operators on approved platforms may run, and **ten vendors** were approved as of 30 January 2026 ([The Citizen, 31 Jan 2026](https://thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/tanzania-gives-bus-operators-14-days-to-adopt-approved-e-ticketing-systems-5344000)). City fares are heading the same way: LATRA is consulting on daladala e-ticketing ([The Citizen, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/tanzania-mulls-e-ticketing-system-for-commuter-buses-5391664)), and the rapid-bus agency has recovered about Sh400 million of the Sh2 billion it put into smart cards ([The Citizen, 20 May 2026](https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/business/dart-explores-biometric-ticketing-system-for-dar-es-salaam-rapid-bus-commuters-5466318)). Uber left Tanzania in January 2026 over regulated fares *[summary]* ([ITWeb Africa](https://itweb.africa/article/uber-pulls-out-of-tanzania-after-losing-ground-to-bolt/lLn14MmQZgdMJ6Aa)).

The open ground is social-media selling. Sellers take orders on Instagram or WhatsApp, confirm payment by screenshot, hand the buyer's number to a boda rider who phones for directions, and struggle with stock, message overload and weak recourse ([The Chanzo, 4 Sep 2026](https://thechanzo.com/2026/09/04/instagram-mobile-money-and-a-boda-tanzanias-e-commerce-economy)). A delivery operator reports customers who stop answering when the rider arrives and goods rejected at the door ([The Citizen, 12 Aug 2026](https://thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/business/how-online-shopping-is-driving-fast-growing-delivery-business-5555032)). All of this is anecdote with no statistics, but no dominant platform was found either. Parcels sent upcountry by bus are tracked by a handwritten reference number and are reported lost *[summary, likely older]* ([Daily News](https://dailynews.co.tz/?p=26323)); that process sits outside the ticketing mandate, though carrying parcels commercially falls under TCRA courier registration.

Rent in Dar es Salaam is paid in blocks: six months up front is the norm and some landlords ask for eight *[summary]* ([The Citizen](https://thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/tanzania-rental-woes-tenants-express-frustrations-as-landlords-increasingly-extend-lease-terms-in-dar-es-salaam-4580898)). Tenants must withhold **10% of rent** and pay it to TRA *[summary]* ([The Guardian/IPP](https://ippmedia.co.tz/the-guardian/news/local-news/read/tenants-retold-to-withhold-remit-10pc-of-rent-to-tra-2025-01-16-191641)). Several small rent apps exist, all known only from their own listings. A generic monthly-invoice rent tool misses local practice; "paid until" dates, renewal reminders and a record of tax withheld fit it. The law on advance rent was not confirmed: a memo citing a three-month cap appears to describe Uganda's law, not Tanzania's *[unconfirmed]*.

Event contributions (michango) are universal, but the operators moved in: Mixx by Yas and Azania Bank launched "Mchango" in February 2026 and Vodacom runs "Changisha" *[summary]* ([The Citizen](https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/mixx-launches-mchango-to-simplify-community-contributions-5349400); [Vodacom FAQ](https://vodacom.co.tz/uploads/FAQ_S_CHANGISHA_Final_907c7f14d1.pdf)). Those products solve collection. The pledge list (who promised, who paid, who needs a reminder) is still a notebook job, and a tool that never touches money is the cleanest first web project on the menu.

## Three rails shape every serious build, and none is open to a learner in production

### Mobile money: an aggregator in production, a mock gateway for learning

Mobile money is the retail payment method: **87.05 million subscriptions in June 2026**, with M-Pesa at 40.6% and the top three operators holding about 89% *[press report of TCRA data]* ([The Citizen](https://thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/mobile-money-surge-fuels-cashless-growth-5578364)). Payments to businesses reached 2.30 billion transactions worth TZS 37.52 trillion in 2025, up 41% by value *[press summary of the Bank of Tanzania report; the report itself was not opened]* ([Daily News](https://dailynews.co.tz/mobile-money-reshapes-economy/)). The government levy on transfers and withdrawals (TZS 10 to 4,000) excludes merchants and businesses *[2022 law-firm note; current bands not retrieved]* ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2022/09/tanzania-national-payment-systems-act-amendments)), so a project that collects payment for a business does not need to model it.

The earlier report said aggregators offer "one API with a sandbox". That needs correcting provider by provider. **ClickPesa has no sandbox**: its own documentation says every request "runs against live production and moves real funds", and API access waits on a business account and KYC ([ClickPesa docs](https://docs.clickpesa.com/)). Selcom, the largest, hands out credentials by email and its documentation mentions no sandbox in the part read ([Selcom Developers](https://developers.selcommobile.com/)). AzamPay has sandbox hosts *[third-party]* ([Centipid docs](https://docs.centipidbilling.com/essentials/payment-azampay)); Tembo and pawaPay document sandboxes ([Tembo](https://tembo.gitbook.io/tembo/reference/mobile-money-collection/momo-collection-flow); [pawaPay](https://docs.pawapay.io/v2/docs/what_to_know)); Vodacom's M-Pesa portal and Airtel's developer portal have sandboxes, but their production access needs a registered Tanzanian business and commercial approval taking weeks *[third-party]* ([McTaba Labs](https://mctaba.com/learn/tanzania/airtel-money-api-integration-tanzania)). Mixx by Yas has no public developer portal that could be found and is reached through aggregators. Whether any sandbox accepts an individual with no company was not confirmed for any provider, and no official fee schedule was found for any of them.

The engineering lessons are consistent across sources. Payment prompts expire in roughly 30 to 120 seconds; callbacks arrive late, twice or never; a timeout is not a failure; and when a callback and a status query disagree, the status query wins *[third-party and provider docs]* ([McTaba Labs](https://mctaba.com/learn/tanzania/common-mobile-money-api-errors-tanzania); [Tembo](https://tembo.gitbook.io/tembo/reference/mobile-money-collection/momo-collection-flow)). Selcom's official rules are the best design reference: for an in-progress or ambiguous result, wait three minutes, query, repeat, and never retry at once; a missed notification does not reverse the money and leaves the transaction for manual reconciliation ([Selcom Developers](https://developers.selcommobile.com/)). Sandboxes do not reproduce these failures (Tembo's triggers no real prompt), so **the most useful thing a learner builds is a mock gateway that deliberately sends late, duplicate and missing callbacks**. Number portability exists in Tanzania ([TCRA numbering FAQ](https://tcra.go.tz/faq/frequent-asked-questions-on-numbering)), so a phone prefix shows only the original operator. That corrects the earlier rule "operator detected from the prefix": the prefix is a default and the user picks the wallet.

National rails are not developer rails. The instant-payment switch (TIPS) and the national QR standard (TANQR) serve licensed banks and e-money issuers; the government payment gateway (GePG) is for public institutions over a private network *[2019 integration document]* ([GePG API v4.0](https://openimis.atlassian.net/wiki/download/attachments/3051225101/Bill+And+++Payment++Information+++PostingAPIv4.0.pdf?version=1)). A learner can still simulate the control-number pattern, and can write a TANQR generator and parser with no account at all, since the standard follows the published EMVCo merchant-presented format ([Bank of Tanzania TANQR guidelines](https://www.bot.go.tz/Publications/Acts,%20Regulations,%20Circulars,%20Guidelines/Guidelines/en/2022081908570183.pdf)).

### SMS and USSD: learn on a simulator; a live sender name needs company paperwork

Local providers are the only sensible route: the only itemised prices found, from 2021, were TZS 13 to 25 per SMS *[third-party, old]* ([DEV post](https://dev.to/alphaolomi/bulk-sms-provider-in-tanzania-1dj4)), against a listed US$0.43 per segment on Twilio *[as read; unusually high, verify]* ([Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/tz)). Every branded sender name must be registered with each network, generic names are refused, and registration takes two to four weeks *[vendor blog]* ([Telerivet](https://www.telerivet.com/blog/sending-sms-in-tanzania-tcra-sender-id-registration-the-pdpa-and-what-operators-need-to-knowtanzania-sms-compliance-tcra-sender-id-pdpa)); Twilio's guidance says unregistered names have been blocked since 16 June 2025 and that registration needs a signed, stamped letter on company letterhead ([Twilio guidelines](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/tz/sms)). A dedicated short code through Africa's Talking costs about US$2,000 to apply and US$3,000 a year plus local fees, while a shared code with a keyword costs TZS 198,500 to set up and TZS 79,400 a month after a KYC form ([Africa's Talking help](http://help.africastalking.com/en/articles/4161210-shortcode-tanzania-2-way-sms)). Africa's Talking is described as offering a sandbox *[third-party]* ([HelloDuty](https://helloduty.com/blogs/getting-started-with-the-africas-talking-ussd-api)), which is enough to learn USSD session handling in full. The earlier report named a Beem sandbox; this round found no evidence for one, so the menu now names Africa's Talking and treats Beem as *[unconfirmed]*. WhatsApp Business Platform replies became chargeable on 1 October 2026 ([The Citizen](https://thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/business/whatsapp-charges-new-fees-to-affect-tanzanian-businesses-5580516)), which keeps SMS competitive for receipts and reminders.

### TRA fiscal receipts: build the whole client against a mock server

A business with annual turnover of TZS 11 million or more must issue fiscal receipts *[summary of TRA page; one outlier source says TZS 14 million]* ([TRA](https://tra.go.tz/page/know-about-e-fiscal-devices-efd)). A Virtual Fiscal Device is software that registers once with TRA using a certificate tied to a TIN, fetches a token, posts signed receipt XML and a daily Z report, and prints a verification QR. **Every technical detail of that flow comes from community libraries, not TRA documentation**: a Go library lists the endpoints, fields and acknowledgement codes, including a test host at `virtual.tra.go.tz` ([Golang-Tanzania tra-vfd](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/Golang-Tanzania/tra-vfd)), and a Laravel package with 8 installs manages receipt counters with row locking and queues receipts when TRA is down ([Packagist](https://root.packagist.org/packages/eyecuejohn/tra-vfd-laravel)). The two libraries disagree on tax-code labels, and how a developer gets test credentials was not found. Becoming an approved VFD supplier reportedly needs **TZS 50 million** in capital *[summary of TRA page]* ([TRA suppliers page](https://www.tra.go.tz/page/efd-vfd-suppliers)), which rules out a solo developer; the practical production route is integration through an approved supplier, though no source showed how suppliers resell access or at what price. Penalties for not issuing a receipt run to 20% of the sale or more *[secondary, summary]* ([VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/?p=355354)). For a learner the conclusion from the earlier report stands and is now better grounded: write a mock TRA service, then build the full client against it. Keeping the global counter, daily counter and Z number correct under concurrent sales and offline queuing is the hard part and the valuable one.

### Data protection, hosting and power set the non-functional rules

The Personal Data Protection Act has been in force since 1 May 2023; the final registration deadline passed on 8 April 2026, with corporate fines reported at TZS 1 million to 5 billion ([The Chanzo](https://thechanzo.com/2026/03/27/data-protection-commission-issues-final-warning-ahead-of-april-enforcement-deadline/)), and on-site inspections were announced from 31 August 2026 *[summary]* ([The Citizen/Nation](https://www.nation.africa/tanzania/news/national/data-protection-regulator-begins-compliance-crackdown-5574710)). Data about children, health and financial transactions is sensitive, and sending personal data abroad needs a permit ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=TZ)). The commission has already awarded TZS 20 million compensation plus a TZS 5 million fine over a child's photo used for business ([Lex Africa](https://lexafrica.com/2025/12/tanzania-data-protection-instagram-ruling/)). Tanzania has local data centres but no hyperscale cloud region, the nearest being in South Africa ([Console Connect](https://blog.consoleconnect.com/console-connect-expands-in-africas-biggest-cloud-hub)), so hosting real personal data abroad raises the permit question. A nationwide blackout on 27 June 2026 ([The Chanzo](https://thechanzo.com/2026/06/29/tanzania-forms-a-team-to-investigate-the-cause-of-nationwide-blackout)) and an internet shutdown that delayed a national cashew auction in late 2025 *[summary]* ([The Citizen](https://thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/business/how-internet-outage-disrupted-cashew-auctions-in-tanzania-5254892)) are the case for light pages, idempotent requests, queues with retries and offline capture.

## Six ideas hold up, seven need narrowing, and nine are learn-only or off-limits

The verdicts below weigh four things: is the problem evidenced, is it already served, does a regulator fence it, and can a learner build it with sandboxes. "Hold up" means build it and it could become a real product. "Narrow it" means the broad idea is crowded or fenced but a smaller version is open. "Build only to learn" means good engineering practice with no route to market for a solo developer. "Avoid" means the evidence shows failure or a licence wall with little learning value to offset it.

| Michango pledge ledger (no money handled) | **Hold up** | Universal practice; operators' products cover collection, not the pledge list | Changisha, Mchango, M-Kadi and others collect money | All *[summary]* or *[vendor]*; no usage numbers |
| AMCOS member, delivery and payment records | **Hold up** | Late payments blamed on wrong bank details; embezzlement cases; 2026 audit orders | No competitor identified; payments themselves run through unions and government systems | Mostly *[summary]*; willingness to pay unknown; no count of AMCOS |
| Order and stock desk for Instagram/WhatsApp sellers | **Hold up** | Manual payment proof, message overload, hard stock control named directly | No dominant platform found | Anecdote from two 2026 features; no market size |

| Farmer price or advice app | **Avoid** | Problem is real | M-Kilimo: 7.2 million registered, about 75,000 consultations | Use by active farmers not measured, but the direction is clear |
| Ride-hailing, daladala or BRT fare collection | **Avoid** | — | Price-regulated; Uber exited; fares moving to regulator-led systems | Driver counts unaudited |
| LUKU electricity vending, group wallet or any app that holds members' money | **Avoid** | — | Runs through government gateway and licensed providers; holding funds triggers BoT licensing | GePG third-party rules not researched |
| Digital lending app for the public | **Avoid** | — | Tier 2 licence, BoT approval, in-country database, data-protection registration | As above |

## The staged menu: five stages, one go-regional exercise, sandboxes throughout

The five-stage structure and the house rules from the earlier report stay. The stage definitions are those of the learning-tracks plan: Stage 1 ends with a tested command-line tool, Stage 2 with a plain-PHP web app with login on a small framework the learner wrote, deployed; Stage 3 with a deployed Laravel app with roles, queues, an API, tests and CI; Stage 4 with a multi-tenant production system with payments and monitoring; Stage 5 with a load-tested service and a written performance report.

**Rules every project follows (revised).** One: bilingual, Kiswahili and English, with Kiswahili as the default; the Bank of Tanzania already requires that of digital lenders ([BoT Guidance Note](https://www.bot.go.tz/Publications/Acts,%20Regulations,%20Circulars,%20Guidelines/Guidelines/en/2024082813141188.pdf)). Use the owner's words (madeni, mauzo, matumizi), but the word list in the notes is *[general knowledge]* and must be checked with real shop owners. Two: light, usable on a cheap phone and a slow link, with no heavy front end before Stage 3. Three: money is exact, stored as whole shillings, every movement recorded and nothing edited in place. Four: phones are identity, stored in one international format, with the operator prefix used only as a default because numbers can be ported. Five: sandbox only, meaning no real money, no real customer data and no real TRA submissions; never practise on ClickPesa, where every call moves real funds. Six: data protection by habit, meaning least data, an access log, export and delete, and no patient or child data unless the project needs it. Seven (new): never hold other people's money; record it, and let a licensed provider move it. Eight (new): assume the power or the network drops mid-request, so make writes idempotent and queue outbound calls.

## What a learner can do without a company, and what needs one

*This section is research, not legal advice. Rules change and several were read only in summaries. Confirm each point with the regulator or a Tanzanian advocate before real users or real money are involved.*

**Without a registered business, a learner can do everything on the staged menu.** That means writing and publishing code, including open-source packages for phone numbers, QR payloads and fiscal receipts; deploying apps that hold only invented data; using the operator and aggregator sandboxes that accept sign-ups (whether each accepts an individual is *[unconfirmed]*, so read the terms at sign-up); learning SMS and USSD on a simulator; and building a full fiscal-receipt client against a mock server. A learner can also give a record-keeping tool to a friend's shop or a savings group to run on their own device, though the moment it stores real people's names and phone numbers the data-protection law applies to whoever controls that data. A practitioner summary says individuals and sole proprietors can register with the commission using a government ID, at a reported small-entity fee of TZS 100,000 *[third-party, 2024; not checked against the fee schedule]* ([Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/regulatory-and-compliance-update-a-guide-to-initial-compliance-with-tanzanias-data-protection-act)).

**A registered business, TIN and business licence are the entry ticket to anything live.** Official guidance sets the order as business name with BRELA, TIN from TRA, then the licence from BRELA or the local authority ([business.go.tz](https://business.go.tz/obtain-a-business-license)), and since 1 July 2026 a person starting a business must apply for a TIN within 15 days ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/07/tanzania-tax-update-finance-act-2026-highlights)). With those in hand a developer can open an aggregator business account and pass KYC to take real payments, register a branded SMS sender name (which needs a letter on company letterhead), rent a shared short code, and register with the data-protection commission as a controller or processor. No aggregator publishes its document list, so the "registered business" requirement rests on third-party guides and ClickPesa's own "business account plus KYC" wording. Tanzania has no startup law in force; the government ordered the policy finished within six months in October 2026 ([TanzaniaInvest](https://www.tanzaniainvest.com/?p=35082)).

**Some things need a licence or regulator approval that a small developer should not expect to get, and should partner for instead.**

| Activity | What it needs | Source and strength |
|---|---|---|
| Holding or moving other people's money (wallets, escrow, payouts) | Bank of Tanzania payment-system or e-money licence; reported minimum capital of TZS 500 million for e-money is *[unconfirmed]* | [BoT regulations](https://www.bot.go.tz/Publications/Acts,%20Regulations,%20Circulars,%20Guidelines/Regulations/en/2020030903230324.pdf); capital figure from a page that could not be opened |
| Lending through an app | Tier 2 microfinance licence plus a BoT no-objection letter; lending without a licence is an offence | [BoT Guidance Note](https://www.bot.go.tz/Publications/Acts,%20Regulations,%20Circulars,%20Guidelines/Guidelines/en/2024082813141188.pdf), read in full; 2025 regulations read in draft |
| Supplying fiscal-receipt software to others | TRA approval as a VFD supplier; reported TZS 50 million capital | [TRA](https://www.tra.go.tz/page/efd-vfd-suppliers) *[summary]* |
| Issuing real fiscal receipts for your own business | A TIN-linked certificate from TRA, or an approved supplier's device | Community libraries; no official specification retrieved |
| Selling bus tickets | LATRA approval; owner must be a government body, cooperative or Tanzania-registered company; integration with LATRA and TRA systems | [The Citizen](https://thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/tanzania-gives-bus-operators-14-days-to-adopt-approved-e-ticketing-systems-5344000), read in full |
| Carrying parcels commercially | TCRA courier registration (for the carrier) | [TCRA procedure](https://www.tcra.go.tz/download/sw-1627477229-Utaratibu%20wa%20Usajili%20wa%20Wasafirishaji%20wa%20VIfurushi%20na%20Vipeto.pdf) *[summary, 2021]* |

| Hosting or sending real personal data outside Tanzania | A permit from the data-protection commission before transfer | [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=TZ); whether routine cloud storage always needs one is a question for the commission |

Three boundaries are unclear and need advice before any launch. The first is where "collecting payment for your own service through a licensed provider" ends and "moving other people's money" begins, for example a marketplace that pays out to sellers. The second is whether an ordinary business web app needs any TCRA licence; the licence categories centre on networks, news and content, but the 2025 and June 2026 amendments were not read. The third is whether a data protection officer is mandatory for every registered controller, on which two law-firm guides disagree. The Electronic Transactions Act and the Cybercrimes Act also apply to online sellers and were not researched; treat them as still to be checked.

## Conclusion

The pattern across every sector is the same, and it is the most useful thing a learner can carry away: in Tanzania the state and the mobile operators own the moment money changes hands, and they are tightening that grip (approved ticketing vendors, a mandatory e-payment Order, lending-app suspensions, registration inspections). What they do not own is the record on either side of that moment, namely the shop's debt book, the committee's pledge list, the group's share-out sheet, the cooperative's delivery register and the seller's order list. Those are where the evidence shows people still losing money and arguing, and where a tool needs no licence. A learner who builds records first and treats payment, SMS and fiscal receipts as interchangeable drivers behind interfaces is practising the exact shape the real market has.

The second lesson is about evidence. The areas with hard numbers are the ones a learner cannot enter, and the open areas rest on anecdote. That makes two or three conversations with a shop owner, a group treasurer or a bursar part of the build, not a courtesy. It also means the mock services are not a workaround. A gateway that misbehaves on purpose and a tax server that rejects a bad signature teach more than any sandbox on offer, and they remain useful as test fixtures on the day a registered business swaps in the real thing.
